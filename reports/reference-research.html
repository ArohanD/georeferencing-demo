Reference evidence and unresolved matching questions

Research date: 2026-09-17. This note establishes candidate identities, not pixel matches or surveyed coordinates. No coordinates have been inferred from town names. Historic symbols must be inspected in the original scan and matched to a documented place, not to label text.

Coordinate conventions

The saved Library of Congress catalog record (data/metadata/loc-item.json, item 83693769) confirms publication by S. Hooper on May 1, 1770, and notes two copies whose coloration differs. Its notes do not establish a prime meridian or modern datum. Searches did not establish a documented Collet-to-WGS84 longitude conversion. Direct inspection of the border inscriptions is necessary. Even a printed London or Greenwich meridian would not establish modern geodetic equivalence or correct historical survey errors. Do not fit modern coordinates to printed graticules by assumption.

The LOC Mouzon catalog explicitly describes that different map's prime meridian as London. This is not evidence for Collet's convention. NCpedia's Collet biography describes Collet as a source for Mouzon's northern portion, so these maps would also be correlated evidence rather than independent validation.

Modern reference data

USGS GNIS is an official feature-name source, but names alone do not establish historical spatial continuity. USGS Circular 1011 defines primary coordinates as approximate centers of area features and mouths of linear features. Consequently a populated-place point is a regional town proxy, not a surveyed 1770 settlement center; a stream primary point can help identify its mouth but still needs visual and historical examination. Record the actual reference dataset, feature identifier, datum and extraction date in any accepted GCP row.

NC HPOWEB 2.0 provides historic resource locations and district boundaries. The service description explicitly excludes archaeological data. Historic district geometry and nomination maps are useful corroboration, not automatic town-center coordinates. Public topographic hydrography can corroborate river junctions; migrating shorelines and inlets require separate historical justification.

Source leads and existing overlays

NCpedia's map excerpt identifies the 1770 Collet map, explicitly calls its displayed image an excerpt, labels it public domain, and links UNC legacy record ncmaps/467. That establishes a real UNC lead; it does not verify either supplied UUID. Both UNC lead 1 and UNC lead 2 failed to open through the browsing tool, and exact-ID search yielded no identity evidence. Record them as unresolved, not Collet.

The three saved ArcGIS item JSON files were inspected:

  • Mouzon item: mouzonimage, owner dloberger, Map Service, description only says georeferenced Mouzon image; no license or attribution field. No GCPs or accuracy evidence established.
  • Moseley item: explicitly 1733 and clipped to a project area. Cites ECU 51135; research/teaching/private-study reuse wording. Not Collet.
  • Cowley/Moseley item: explicitly 1737, georeferencing attributed to Wake Forest CHARG. Describes a color copy of a manuscript in private ownership, presented by NC State Archives for research/education; commercial use requires prior permission. Not an original Collet scan.

Item identity and descriptive metadata were verified; service tile accuracy, transformations, and GCPs were not. None should serve as independent ground truth for the Collet pilot.
